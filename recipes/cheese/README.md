## Essential Equipment

### Basic
- Large stainless steel pot (4+ gallons for most recipes)
- Thermometer (accurate to within 2°F)
- Cheesecloth or butter muslin
- Colander or cheese molds
- Non-iodized salt (cheese salt or kosher salt)

### For Pressed Cheeses
- Cheese press (or DIY with weights)
- Hard cheese molds (tomme, Gouda, etc.)
- pH meter or strips (helpful but optional)

### For Aged Cheeses
- Aging cave or dedicated refrigerator (50-55°F)
- Humidity control (90-95% for most)
- Cheese mats and turning boards
- Brine container

## Key Ingredients

### Milk
- **Whole milk**: Best for most recipes
- **Raw milk**: Most flavorful (where legal)
- **Pasteurized**: Works well, avoid ultra-pasteurized
- **Sheep/Goat**: Traditional for many European cheeses

### Cultures
- **Mesophilic**: For cheeses made below 102°F
- **Thermophilic**: For higher-temperature cheeses
- **Penicillium candidum**: White bloomy rinds
- **Penicillium roqueforti**: Blue veining

### Coagulants
- **Liquid rennet**: Most common, easy to measure
- **Vegetable rennet**: Vegetarian alternative
- **Citric acid**: Quick curds for mozzarella
- **Tartaric acid**: Clean flavor for mascarpone

## Tips for Success

1. **Temperature is critical** - Invest in a good thermometer
2. **Don't rush** - Let curds set fully before cutting
3. **Sanitation matters** - Clean everything that touches the cheese
4. **Save your whey** - Use for baking, ricotta, or animal feed
5. **Start simple** - Master fresh cheeses before aged ones
6. **Keep records** - Note what works for future batches
7. **Humidity control** - Essential for successful aging
