# Homemade Camembert

**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Time:** 3 hours active + 3-4 weeks aging
**Yield:** Two 4-inch rounds (about 8 oz each)

## Description
Camembert is a soft, creamy French cheese from Normandy with a white bloomy rind. Its rich, earthy flavor intensifies as it ages, becoming runny and complex. This is one of the most rewarding cheeses to make at home.

## Ingredients

### For the Cheese
- 2 gallons whole milk (not ultra-pasteurized)
- 1/8 teaspoon mesophilic starter culture
- 1/16 teaspoon Penicillium candidum (white mold)
- 1/16 teaspoon Geotrichum candidum (optional, for better rind)
- 1/4 teaspoon calcium chloride in 1/4 cup water
- 1/4 teaspoon liquid rennet dissolved in 1/4 cup cool water
- 2 teaspoons cheese salt (non-iodized)

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot
- Thermometer
- Long knife
- Slotted spoon
- Ladle
- Two 4-inch Camembert molds
- Cheese mat
- Aging container with lid (plastic box works well)

## Instructions

### Day 1: Make the Curds

#### Step 1: Culture the Milk
1. Heat milk to 90°F (32°C)
2. Add calcium chloride and stir
3. Sprinkle mesophilic culture and mold powders on surface
4. Let rehydrate 2 minutes, then stir in
5. Cover and ripen at 90°F for 90 minutes

#### Step 2: Add Rennet
1. Stir in rennet solution gently for 1 minute
2. Cover and let set for 90 minutes at 90°F
3. Test for clean break

#### Step 3: Cut and Rest
1. Cut curds into 1/2-inch cubes
2. Let rest 5 minutes
3. Gently stir for 10 minutes
4. Let curds settle 5 minutes

#### Step 4: Mold the Cheese
1. Place molds on a draining mat over a tray
2. Ladle curds gently into molds - fill to the top
3. Let drain 2 hours, then add more curds as they settle
4. Continue until all curds are used
5. Let drain at room temperature (68-72°F) overnight

### Day 2: Flip and Salt

#### Step 5: Unmold and Salt
1. Flip cheeses onto clean mat every 2-3 hours for 24 hours
2. Once firm enough to handle (usually after 24 hours), unmold
3. Salt all surfaces lightly - about 1/2 tsp per cheese
4. Place on clean mat in aging container
5. Leave lid slightly open for air circulation

### Days 3-14: Initial Aging

#### Step 6: Develop the Rind
1. Age at 50-55°F with 90-95% humidity
2. Flip daily
3. White mold should appear within 5-7 days
4. By day 10-14, cheese should be covered in white fuzz

### Weeks 3-4: Final Aging

#### Step 7: Wrap and Ripen
1. Once fully covered in white mold, wrap in cheese paper
2. Continue aging at 50-55°F
3. Check weekly - cheese should soften from outside in
4. Ready when it feels soft throughout (3-4 weeks total)
5. For runnier cheese, age longer (5-6 weeks)

## Storage
- Ripe Camembert keeps 1-2 weeks in refrigerator
- Store wrapped in cheese paper, not plastic wrap
- Bring to room temperature 30 minutes before serving

## Tips
- Humidity is critical - too dry and the rind cracks
- If blue/green mold appears, wipe with salt solution
- The ammonia smell is normal as it ripens
- Cut when the center is slightly firm for best slicing

## Serving Suggestions
- Baked Camembert with garlic and herbs
- On crusty bread with fig jam
- With apple slices and walnuts
- Melted over roasted vegetables
