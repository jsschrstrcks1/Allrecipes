# Homemade Chèvre (Fresh Goat Cheese)

**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Time:** 30 minutes active + 24 hours draining
**Yield:** About 1 lb

## Description
Chèvre is a fresh, tangy French goat cheese with a creamy, spreadable texture. Its bright, citrusy flavor makes it versatile for both sweet and savory dishes. This is one of the easiest cheeses to make at home.

## Ingredients
- 1 gallon fresh goat's milk (not ultra-pasteurized)
- 1 packet direct-set chèvre culture OR:
  - 1/8 teaspoon mesophilic culture
  - 2 drops liquid rennet in 2 tbsp water
- 1 teaspoon cheese salt (non-iodized)
- Fresh herbs (optional): chives, thyme, dill

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot
- Thermometer
- Long spoon
- Cheesecloth or butter muslin
- Colander
- String for hanging

## Instructions

### Step 1: Heat the Milk
1. Pour goat's milk into pot
2. Heat slowly to 86°F (30°C)
3. Stir occasionally to prevent scorching

### Step 2: Add Culture
1. Sprinkle culture on milk surface
2. Let rehydrate 2 minutes
3. Stir gently to incorporate
4. If using rennet, add now and stir briefly

### Step 3: Incubate
1. Cover pot
2. Let sit at room temperature for 12-24 hours
3. Milk will thicken to yogurt-like consistency
4. A layer of whey may form on top

### Step 4: Drain
1. Line colander with damp cheesecloth
2. Gently ladle curds into cloth
3. Let drain 1 hour in colander
4. For firmer cheese, tie corners and hang
5. Drain 12-24 hours depending on desired texture

### Step 5: Season
1. Transfer drained cheese to bowl
2. Add salt and mix well
3. Add herbs if desired
4. Taste and adjust seasoning

### Step 6: Shape
**Log**: Roll in plastic wrap, twist ends
**Rounds**: Press into small molds
**Crumbled**: Use as-is

## Variations

### Herbed Chèvre
- 2 tbsp fresh chopped herbs (any combination)
- Mix into finished cheese

### Honey Lavender Chèvre
- 2 tbsp honey
- 1 tsp dried culinary lavender (crushed)
- Mix honey in; roll log in lavender

### Peppered Chèvre
- 1 tbsp cracked black pepper
- Roll finished log in pepper

### Ash-Coated Chèvre
- 2 tbsp vegetable ash (food grade)
- Roll or dust finished cheese with ash

## Storage
- Wrap tightly in plastic or wax paper
- Refrigerate up to 2 weeks
- Flavor intensifies after 2-3 days

## Tips
- Fresh goat's milk makes the best chèvre
- Pasteurized works, but avoid ultra-pasteurized
- Longer draining = firmer, tangier cheese
- The whey is great for baking or smoothies

## Serving Suggestions
- Spread on crostini with honey
- Crumbled on salads
- Stuffed in chicken breasts
- On pizza with fig and prosciutto
- Whipped into pasta
- With roasted beets
