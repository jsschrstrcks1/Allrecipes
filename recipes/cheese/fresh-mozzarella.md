# Fresh Mozzarella

**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Time:** 1 hour
**Yield:** About 1 pound

## Description
Fresh mozzarella is a soft, mild Italian cheese with a delicate milky flavor. Perfect for caprese salads, pizza, and sandwiches.

## Ingredients
- 1 gallon whole milk (not ultra-pasteurized)
- 1.5 teaspoons citric acid dissolved in 1 cup cool water
- 1/4 teaspoon liquid rennet dissolved in 1/4 cup cool water
- 1 teaspoon cheese salt (non-iodized)

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot (6+ quarts)
- Thermometer
- Long knife or curd cutter
- Slotted spoon
- Microwave-safe bowl
- Rubber gloves

## Instructions

### Step 1: Acidify the Milk
1. Pour citric acid solution into the pot
2. Add cold milk and stir thoroughly
3. Heat slowly to 90°F (32°C), stirring gently

### Step 2: Add Rennet
1. Remove pot from heat
2. Stir in rennet solution using up-and-down motions for 30 seconds
3. Cover and let sit undisturbed for 5 minutes
4. Check for a clean break - the curd should look like firm custard

### Step 3: Cut and Cook the Curds
1. Cut the curd into 1-inch cubes using a long knife
2. Return to heat and slowly raise temperature to 105°F (40°C)
3. Stir gently as you heat - curds will shrink and become firmer
4. Remove from heat when curds are soft but hold their shape

### Step 4: Drain the Curds
1. Use a slotted spoon to transfer curds to a microwave-safe bowl
2. Press curds gently to release whey
3. Drain off the whey (save it for ricotta!)

### Step 5: Stretch the Cheese
1. Microwave curds for 1 minute
2. Drain any released whey
3. Knead the curds like bread dough (wear gloves - it's hot!)
4. Microwave for 35 seconds, then knead again
5. Add salt and continue kneading
6. Microwave for 35 more seconds if needed
7. Stretch and fold until smooth, shiny, and elastic

### Step 6: Shape and Store
1. Shape into balls, logs, or braids
2. Place in ice water for 5 minutes to set the shape
3. Store in salted whey or water in the refrigerator
4. Best consumed within 3-5 days

## Tips
- Milk temperature is crucial - use a good thermometer
- If curds won't stretch, they need more heat
- Over-kneading makes the cheese rubbery
- The whey can be used to make ricotta cheese

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Curds won't form | Milk too cold or rennet inactive | Ensure 90°F temp, use fresh rennet |
| Cheese is rubbery | Over-stretched or too hot | Reduce kneading time |
| Cheese is grainy | Not enough acid | Add more citric acid next time |
| Cheese falls apart | Not enough heat when stretching | Microwave longer |
