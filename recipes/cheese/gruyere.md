# Homemade Gruyère

**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Time:** 5 hours active + 5-12 months aging
**Yield:** About 4 lbs

## Description
Gruyère is a Swiss alpine cheese known for its complex, nutty, slightly sweet flavor. Essential for fondue and French onion soup, this firm cheese develops small holes and a deep, earthy character with extended aging.

## Ingredients
- 4 gallons whole milk (raw milk traditional, pasteurized works)
- 1/4 teaspoon thermophilic starter culture
- 1/8 teaspoon Propionibacterium shermanii (for eyes - optional)
- 1/2 teaspoon calcium chloride in 1/4 cup water
- 1 teaspoon liquid rennet dissolved in 1/4 cup cool water
- Cheese salt for brining

### For the Brine
- 2 lbs cheese salt
- 1 gallon water
- 1 tablespoon calcium chloride
- 1 teaspoon white vinegar

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot (6+ gallons)
- Thermometer
- Long knife and curd harp
- Large slotted spoon
- Alpine/tomme cheese mold (8-inch)
- Heavy-duty cheese press
- Aging cave or dedicated refrigerator

## Instructions

### Step 1: Culture the Milk
1. Heat milk to 90°F (32°C)
2. Add calcium chloride, stir well
3. Sprinkle thermophilic culture on surface
4. Add Propionibacterium shermanii if using
5. Let rehydrate 2 minutes, stir in
6. Cover and ripen 15 minutes

### Step 2: Add Rennet
1. Add rennet solution
2. Stir gently for 1 minute
3. Cover and let set 30-40 minutes
4. Test for clean break

### Step 3: Cut Curds Small
1. Cut curds into 1/4-inch cubes (rice-sized)
2. Use curd harp for fine cutting
3. Let rest 5 minutes
4. Stir gently for 10 minutes

### Step 4: Cook the Curds
1. Slowly raise temperature to 120°F (49°C) over 45 minutes
2. Stir constantly to prevent matting
3. Curds should shrink significantly
4. Continue stirring at 120°F for 30 more minutes
5. Curds should be small and springy

### Step 5: Mold and Press
1. Drain whey
2. Transfer curds quickly to warm, cloth-lined mold
3. Press at 10 lbs for 15 minutes
4. Flip, press at 25 lbs for 30 minutes
5. Flip, press at 40 lbs for 2 hours
6. Flip, press at 50 lbs overnight

### Step 6: Make and Use Brine
1. Dissolve salt in water (saturated solution)
2. Add calcium chloride and vinegar
3. Chill to 55°F
4. Brine cheese for 12 hours per pound
5. Flip once during brining

### Step 7: Initial Aging (Weeks 1-4)
1. Remove from brine, pat dry
2. Age at 55°F, 90% humidity
3. Turn daily for first week
4. Wash with brine solution 2-3 times per week
5. Rind will develop golden color

### Step 8: Warm Room (Weeks 5-8)
1. Move to 65-68°F for 3-4 weeks
2. This develops the eyes (holes)
3. Continue turning and washing
4. Watch for slight swelling as eyes form

### Step 9: Final Aging (Months 3-12)
1. Return to 55°F, 85% humidity
2. Turn weekly
3. Continue occasional brine washes
4. Minimum 5 months for table cheese
5. 10-12 months for complex, nutty flavor

## Storage
- Whole wheel: Cool cellar or refrigerator
- Cut pieces: Wrap in wax paper, refrigerate
- Grated: Freezes well

## Tips
- Small curds = firm cheese suitable for aging
- The warm room phase creates the characteristic eyes
- Brine washing develops the distinctive rind
- Longer aging = more intense flavor and crystals

## Classic Uses
- Cheese fondue (with Emmental)
- French onion soup
- Croque monsieur
- Quiche Lorraine
- Gratins
- Alpine cheese board with dried fruits
