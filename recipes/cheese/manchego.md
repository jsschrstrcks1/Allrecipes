# Homemade Manchego-Style Cheese

**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Time:** 4 hours active + 2-12 months aging
**Yield:** About 2 lbs

## Description
Manchego is Spain's most famous cheese, traditionally made from Manchega sheep's milk in the La Mancha region. It has a distinctive herringbone pattern on the rind and a rich, nutty, slightly tangy flavor that intensifies with age.

## Ingredients
- 2 gallons sheep's milk (or substitute goat/cow milk blend)
- 1/4 teaspoon thermophilic starter culture
- 1/4 teaspoon calcium chloride in 1/4 cup water
- 1/2 teaspoon liquid rennet dissolved in 1/4 cup cool water
- 2 tablespoons cheese salt

### For the Brine
- 1 lb cheese salt
- 1/2 gallon water

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot
- Thermometer
- Long knife
- Slotted spoon
- Manchego mold with herringbone pattern (or plain tomme mold)
- Cheese press
- Aging cave or refrigerator

## Instructions

### Step 1: Culture the Milk
1. Heat milk to 86°F (30°C)
2. Add calcium chloride, stir well
3. Sprinkle thermophilic culture on surface
4. Let rehydrate 2 minutes, stir in
5. Cover and ripen at 86°F for 30 minutes

### Step 2: Add Rennet
1. Raise temperature to 90°F (32°C)
2. Add rennet solution, stir gently
3. Cover and let set 30-45 minutes
4. Test for clean break

### Step 3: Cut and Cook
1. Cut curds into 1/4-inch cubes
2. Let rest 5 minutes
3. Slowly heat to 104°F (40°C) over 30 minutes
4. Stir gently and continuously
5. Curds should shrink and firm up

### Step 4: Mold and Press
1. Drain whey
2. Transfer curds to mold
3. Press at 8 lbs for 20 minutes
4. Flip, press at 15 lbs for 30 minutes
5. Flip, press at 25 lbs for 8 hours
6. Flip, press at 25 lbs for 8 more hours

### Step 5: Brine
1. Make saturated brine solution
2. Chill to 50-55°F
3. Brine cheese for 6 hours per pound
4. Flip halfway through

### Step 6: Age
1. Remove from brine, pat dry
2. Air-dry at room temperature 2-3 days
3. Rub with olive oil (traditional)
4. Age at 55°F, 85% humidity

### Aging Times
- **Semi-curado (2-3 months)**: Mild, creamy, subtle tang
- **Curado (3-6 months)**: Firmer, nuttier, more complex
- **Viejo (6-12 months)**: Hard, intense, crystalline texture
- **Añejo (12+ months)**: Sharp, crumbly, for grating

## Storage
- Whole wheel: Cool, dark place or refrigerator
- Cut: Wrap in wax paper, then plastic; refrigerate
- Grated: Freezes well for 3 months

## Tips
- Sheep's milk gives authentic flavor
- The herringbone pattern mold is traditional but optional
- Olive oil rub creates traditional dark rind
- Higher cooking temperature = firmer cheese

## Serving Suggestions
- Sliced with quince paste (membrillo)
- With Marcona almonds
- On a Spanish cheese board
- Shaved over salads
- With Serrano ham
- Aged Manchego with sherry
