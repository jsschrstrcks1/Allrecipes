# Homemade Paneer

**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Time:** 45 minutes (plus pressing time)
**Yield:** About 12 oz (350g)

## Description
Paneer is a fresh, non-melting Indian cheese. Its firm texture makes it perfect for curries like palak paneer and saag paneer, as well as grilling and frying.

## Ingredients
- 1/2 gallon whole milk
- 3 tablespoons lemon juice, lime juice, or white vinegar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt (optional)

## Equipment
- Large pot
- Thermometer
- Fine-mesh strainer or colander
- Cheesecloth (butter muslin works best)
- Heavy weight for pressing (cans, books, or a pot of water)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Heat the Milk
1. Pour milk into a large pot
2. Heat over medium-high heat, stirring frequently to prevent scorching
3. Bring to a boil (212°F / 100°C)
4. Watch carefully - milk boils over quickly!

### Step 2: Curdle the Milk
1. Reduce heat to low
2. Add lemon juice one tablespoon at a time, stirring gently
3. The milk should curdle immediately, with clear greenish whey separating
4. If still milky, add more acid 1 teaspoon at a time
5. Remove from heat once fully curdled

### Step 3: Drain the Curds
1. Line strainer with cheesecloth
2. Pour the curds and whey through the strainer
3. Rinse curds under cool water to remove acidic taste
4. Add salt if desired and mix gently

### Step 4: Press the Paneer
1. Gather the cheesecloth and twist to form a tight bundle
2. Press out as much liquid as possible
3. Place the wrapped paneer on a flat surface
4. Put a flat plate or cutting board on top
5. Add 15-20 lbs of weight (gallon jugs of water work well)
6. Press for 1-2 hours:
   - 1 hour: Softer paneer for crumbling
   - 2 hours: Firm paneer for cubing

### Step 5: Store
1. Unwrap and cut into cubes
2. Store in water in the refrigerator (change water daily)
3. Use within 1 week

## Cooking Methods

### Pan-Fried Paneer
1. Cut into 1-inch cubes
2. Heat oil in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat
3. Fry until golden on all sides (2-3 minutes per side)
4. Season with salt and spices

### Grilled Paneer
1. Cut into thick slices or large cubes
2. Brush with oil and season with spices
3. Grill over medium heat until char marks appear

### Paneer for Curry
1. Fry cubes until golden
2. Add to curry at the end of cooking
3. Simmer just 5 minutes to absorb flavors

## Tips
- Use full-fat milk for best texture
- Don't overheat after adding acid - curds become rubbery
- Paneer doesn't melt, so it's perfect for high-heat cooking
- For softer paneer (like for bhurji), skip pressing

## Classic Dishes
- Palak Paneer (spinach curry)
- Paneer Tikka (grilled skewers)
- Mattar Paneer (pea curry)
- Paneer Bhurji (scrambled paneer)
- Shahi Paneer (creamy tomato curry)
