# Queso Fresco

**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Time:** 1 hour
**Yield:** About 1 pound

## Description
Queso fresco ("fresh cheese") is a mild, crumbly Mexican cheese. It softens when heated but doesn't melt, making it perfect for tacos, enchiladas, and salads.

## Ingredients
- 1 gallon whole milk
- 1/4 cup white vinegar or lime juice
- 1 teaspoon salt

## Equipment
- Large pot
- Thermometer
- Fine-mesh strainer
- Cheesecloth
- Cheese mold or small container with holes

## Instructions

### Step 1: Heat the Milk
1. Pour milk into a large pot
2. Heat slowly over medium heat to 180°F (82°C)
3. Stir occasionally to prevent scorching

### Step 2: Add Acid
1. Remove from heat
2. Slowly stir in vinegar or lime juice
3. Curds will form immediately, separating from cloudy whey
4. If whey is still milky, add 1 more tablespoon acid
5. Let sit for 10 minutes

### Step 3: Drain
1. Line strainer with cheesecloth
2. Pour curds and whey through strainer
3. Let drain for 5 minutes
4. Add salt and mix gently

### Step 4: Press
1. Gather cheesecloth and squeeze out excess whey
2. Place in a cheese mold or shape into a disk
3. Press with light weight (2-5 lbs) for 30 minutes
4. Alternatively, tie cheesecloth and hang to drain

### Step 5: Finish
1. Remove from mold
2. Wrap in plastic wrap
3. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour before using
4. Best within 1 week

## Variations

### Queso Fresco with Herbs
Add 2 tablespoons fresh chopped cilantro or epazote to the curds

### Queso Fresco with Chiles
Mix in 1-2 tablespoons minced jalapeño

### Queso Fresco with Lime
Use lime juice as the acid and add lime zest for extra brightness

## Tips
- Lime juice gives more authentic flavor than vinegar
- Don't over-press or cheese becomes rubbery
- Crumble just before serving for best texture
- If too bland, increase salt slightly

## Uses
- Crumbled on tacos and tostadas
- Topping for elote (Mexican street corn)
- Inside enchiladas
- Crumbled on black beans
- Topping for chilaquiles
- Stuffed in chiles rellenos
- Garnish for soups like pozole
