# Homemade Roquefort-Style Blue Cheese

**Difficulty:** Advanced
**Time:** 4 hours active + 3-6 months aging
**Yield:** About 3 lbs

## Description
Roquefort is the "King of Cheeses" - a tangy, crumbly blue cheese traditionally made from raw sheep's milk and aged in the limestone caves of southern France. This recipe creates a similar style blue cheese that can be made with cow's or sheep's milk.

## Ingredients

### For the Cheese
- 3 gallons whole milk (sheep's milk traditional, cow's milk works)
- 1/4 teaspoon mesophilic starter culture
- 1/8 teaspoon Penicillium roqueforti
- 1/2 teaspoon calcium chloride in 1/4 cup water
- 1/2 teaspoon liquid rennet dissolved in 1/4 cup cool water
- 3 tablespoons cheese salt (non-iodized)

## Equipment
- Large stainless steel pot
- Thermometer
- Long knife and curd harp
- Slotted spoon
- Blue cheese molds with drainage holes
- Cheese needles or skewers for piercing
- Aging cave or refrigerator setup

## Instructions

### Day 1: Make the Curds

#### Step 1: Culture the Milk
1. Heat milk to 86°F (30°C)
2. Add calcium chloride, stir well
3. Sprinkle mesophilic culture on surface
4. Add Penicillium roqueforti
5. Let rehydrate 2 minutes, then stir
6. Cover and ripen at 86°F for 60 minutes

#### Step 2: Add Rennet
1. Add rennet solution, stir gently
2. Cover and let set for 90 minutes at 86°F
3. Test for clean break

#### Step 3: Cut Curds
1. Cut curds into 1/2-inch cubes
2. Let rest 5 minutes
3. Gently stir for 30 minutes
4. Let curds settle 10 minutes
5. Drain off whey to curd level

#### Step 4: Mold
1. Ladle curds into mold loosely - don't compress
2. Air pockets help blue veining develop
3. Let drain at room temperature
4. Flip every 30 minutes for first 3 hours
5. Continue draining overnight at 68-72°F

### Day 2-3: Salt

#### Step 5: Dry Salting
1. Remove from mold
2. Rub salt on all surfaces
3. Place on mat, flip twice daily
4. Continue salting over 3 days
5. Use about 1 tbsp salt per day

### Day 4: Pierce

#### Step 6: Create Air Channels
1. Using cheese needles or metal skewers
2. Pierce cheese from top to bottom (20-30 holes)
3. Pierce from side to side (20-30 holes)
4. These holes allow oxygen for blue mold growth

### Weeks 1-4: Initial Aging

#### Step 7: Develop Blue Veins
1. Age at 50°F with 95% humidity
2. Turn twice weekly
3. Blue veins should appear in 2-3 weeks
4. Pierce again at week 2 if needed

### Months 2-6: Cave Aging

#### Step 8: Final Ripening
1. Continue at 45-50°F, 95% humidity
2. Turn weekly
3. Scrape off any unwanted surface mold
4. Minimum 2 months, better at 4-6 months
5. Cheese develops more pungent flavor with age

## Storage
- Wrap tightly in foil
- Refrigerate - keeps 3-4 weeks once cut
- Can freeze for up to 6 months (texture changes slightly)

## Tips
- Loose curds = more air = better blue veining
- High humidity is essential for blue mold
- Too dry = no blue development
- The blue mold is perfectly safe to eat
- If too salty, age longer or soak slices briefly in milk

## Flavor Development
- 2 months: Mild, creamy, hint of blue
- 3 months: Balanced, tangy, visible veins
- 4-6 months: Strong, pungent, crumbly
- Beyond 6 months: Intense, sharp, connoisseur style

## Classic Uses
- Salad dressings and dips
- Crumbled on steak
- With pears and walnuts
- In pasta sauces
- On a cheese board with Port wine
